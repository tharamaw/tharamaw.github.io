Activism and philanthropy

Diversity and anti-racism

Hamilton is a prominent advocate against racism and for increased diversity in motorsport.[330][331] In 2011—after being summoned to the stewards in five of the opening six rounds—Hamilton quipped, "maybe it's because I'm black, that's what Ali G says."[332] In 2018, Hamilton criticised the lack of diversity in Formula One, stating "nothing's changed" in the 11 years since his debut.[333][334]As part of the U.S. national anthem protests, Hamilton took the knee before every Grand Prix he entered in 2020, in support of Black Lives Matter, and wore t-shirts promoting racial justice.[335] Following the murder of George Floyd in May 2020, Hamilton criticised prominent figures in Formula One for their silence on the issue, writing on Instagram:I see those of you who are staying silent, some of you the biggest of stars [sic] yet you stay silent in the midst of injustice. Not a sign from anybody in my industry which of course is a white dominated sport. I'm one of the only people of colour there yet I stand alone. [...] I would have thought by now you would see why this happens and say something about it but you can't stand alongside us. Just know I know who you are and I see you. [...] We are not born with racism and hate in our hearts, it is taught by those we look up to.[336][337][338]Following Hamilton's comments, several drivers and team executives reciprocated his views.[339][340] Ross Brawn, managing director for Formula One, stated that the organisation "supports [Hamilton] totally", describing Hamilton as "a great ambassador for the sport".[341] During the 2020 Tuscan Grand Prix weekend, including on the podium, Hamilton wore a t-shirt stating "arrest the cops who killed Breonna Taylor". Following an investigation, the FIA barred drivers from wearing unofficial attire on the podium and in media interviews.[342] In anticipation of the FIA's decision, Hamilton said that he recognised that they have "certain limits that they feel that they have to work within", but he "[didn't] regret a single moment of it" and appreciated the "really positive support from the fans".[331]In June 2020, Hamilton established The Hamilton Commission with the Royal Academy of Engineering, tasked with finding ways for motorsport to engage more black people with STEM subjects and increase employment in motorsport or other engineering sectors.[343][344] In May 2021, Hamilton became the inaugural recipient of the Laureus Athlete Advocate of the Year Award.[345] Hamilton founded Mission 44 in July 2021, a charity designed to help young people from under-represented backgrounds achieve their ambitions in wider society. Hamilton pledged £20 million to support the work of the charity.[346] Mission 44 collaborates with a joint foundation between Hamilton and Mercedes called Ignite, which was also launched in July.[347] Ignite focuses on increasing diversity in motorsport by improving opportunities in education and offering financial support.[348]


Human rights

In December 2020, Hamilton confronted human rights in Bahrain and spoke out on allegations of sportswashing. Hamilton said he "won't let it go unnoticed" after an 11-year-old boy wrote a letter to Hamilton asking him to save his father, who was facing the death penalty after making an alleged forced confession.[349][350] Hamilton discussed the case with human rights organisations and legal experts, as well as Bahraini officials. He stated that whilst he has no authority over where he competes, drivers should not "[go] to these countries and just [ignore] what is happening".[351]Hamilton is also a supporter of LGBT rights, and strongly criticised the Hungarian government before the 2021 Hungarian Grand Prix for their anti-LGBT law.[352] He also called out LGBT rights in Saudi Arabia before the inaugural Saudi Arabian Grand Prix, describing them as "terrifying".[353]


Environmental and animal rights

Hamilton has discussed environmental issues and animal rights in press conferences, interviews and documentaries.[354][better source needed][355] He has also used his social media platforms to gather support for his initiatives,[356][better source needed] which have included urging China to reclassify dogs as pets instead of livestock,[357][better source needed] backing charities against wildlife smuggling,[358] and calling for the protection of the Amazon rainforest.[359]In January 2020, Hamilton donated US$500,000 to causes relating to bushfires in Australia.[360] He later announced his aim of being carbon neutral by the end of the year, explaining that he had mandated that his office and household use recyclable items, sold his private jet, and planned to reduce his use of aircraft.[361]


UNICEF and TOGETHERBAND

In 2012, Hamilton began working with the United Nations Children's Fund (UNICEF). In March 2012, Hamilton travelled to the Philippines where he made a short film about Manila's street children. The film was shown on ITV1 during Soccer Aid and helped raise over £4.9 million for UNICEF.[362][363][364] In October 2012, while in India for the Grand Prix, Hamilton visited a UNICEF-funded newborn care unit and nutrition centre. Hamilton said: "As a sportsman in the public eye, I know I have a role to play in helping to tell the stories of the world's most vulnerable children and I jumped at the chance to be able to do that again after a visit to Manila."[365][366][367]In 2014, Hamilton travelled to Haiti where he made a short film about child malnutrition. The film was shown on ITV1 during Soccer Aid, and helped raise over £6 million for UNICEF.[368][369][370] In 2015, Hamilton took part in an exhibition to highlight UNICEF's work and to celebrate its twenty-year partnership with Starwood Hotels.[371][372] In June 2017, Hamilton joined the Super Dads initiative, a special UNICEF campaign that highlighted the critical role played by fathers in early childhood development.[373][374][375] In August 2017, Hamilton visited Havana with UNICEF to learn more about its first development programmes in Cuba.[376][377]In 2020, Hamilton partnered with charity campaign #TOGETHERBAND to help promote the United Nations' 17 Global Goals. As part of his campaign work, Hamilton visited Alperton Community School in North West London to speak to the students about the importance of education. Hamilton is a GOAL 4 Ambassador, focusing on the fight to provide quality education to all children.[378]


Other charities

Over the past decade, Hamilton has made time for a variety of causes, such as making donations and hospital visits to sick children.[379][380][381] He has invited fans, young people,[382][383] and their families to join him at Grand Prix races and social events.[384][385][386] In 2013, he became the Global Education Ambassador for Save the Children, supporting and promoting its education campaigns.[387][388][389] Two years later, Hamilton became the first ambassador for the Invictus Games Foundation, supporting wounded, injured, and sick servicemen and women.[390][391][392] During the COVID-19 pandemic, Hamilton's Neat Burger restaurant donated free meals to frontline NHS workers.[393][394] Neat Burger also launched the "Kids Eat Free" scheme, serving free meals to school children during the half-term break.[395][396]Hamilton often donates personal and professional paraphernalia for charity auctions.[397][398][399] He auctioned a racing kart and raised over £42,000 for St Thomas' Hospital baby charity.[27] He raised £6,411 for the Small Steps Project in 2018, and £6,000 in 2019.[400] In 2020, he raised another £4,000 for the Small Steps Project, while a donated race suit reportedly raised €20,000 for vulnerable children.[401] Hamilton also frequently attends charitable functions,[402][403] including the amfAR gala in New York,[404][405] and has supported projects and charities such as the Make-A-Wish Foundation,[406] Comic Relief,[407] Rays of Sunshine,[408] Children in Need,[409] and Stevenage's Keech Hospice Care Children's Service, among others.[410] He is also involved in charitable work through the creation of his Lewis Hamilton Foundation. Registered in June 2008, the foundation provides grants and donations to a number of charitable causes.[411][412]
